# A typical sol at Outpost Gale

Draft, 13 Nov 2026. The systems in this page are built before the event; PIP's exact lines, the cards' words and the
order of the sols are the storyline, written at the event (workspace hard rule 1). Every number a player sees needs a
row in `research/junior-astronaut/factcheck.md`; the ones still missing are listed at the end.

## 1. The idea in one line 🧭
**Feel it first, then name it.** A kid jumps and floats, and only then does PIP say "That's Mars gravity". Other games
teach a word ("gravity: 0.38 g"). We give the body the experience, then the word sticks to it.

Three rules every scene follows:
1. **Do, then name.** No lesson starts with words. The kid acts, something surprising happens, PIP names it in one line.
2. **Every choice costs something** (the brief). Power to the heater is power not making oxygen.
3. **Real consequence, never a fake timer.** Things change because Mars is like that (dust settles, nights are cold,
   messages take minutes), never because a bar says "wait 4 h or pay".

What we are not: a base builder with timers and gems. Nothing is farmed for its own sake, and every task exists because a
real crew would do it.

## 2. The first five minutes (Sol 1, dawn) 🌅
Judges play about five minutes, so the wow, the first lesson and the first choice all land here.

| Time | What happens | What the kid feels | What PIP names |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Black, then breathing in a helmet. The airlock finishes its cycle with a hiss, the outer hatch slides open on the dawn. Zen music | "I'm really there" | nothing yet |
| 0:20 | The kid steps out. Mount Sharp on the horizon, long shadows, dust in the light. Footsteps are faint outside, loud inside | Mars is quiet and huge | "Air here is very thin. Sound fades fast." |
| 0:45 | PIP rolls up, waves: "Good morning, Commander!" It asks the kid to walk to it, then look around (the controls, taught by doing) | The controls | "Walk to me." |
| 1:30 | "Try a jump!" The hero floats up and comes down slowly. A faint ghost shows the same jump on Earth, half as high | Lightness | "You weigh less here. Mars pulls 0.38 as hard as Earth." |
| 2:15 | A cargo bag the size of the hero: "Can you lift that?" The kid can | Strength, surprise | "On Earth that's too heavy. Here, easy." |
| 3:00 | The sun is up. The solar wings turn to face it. Power meter climbs | The base is alive | "Sunlight is our power. Mars gets less than half of Earth's." |
| 3:45 | First choice: PIP shows the oxygen maker and the greenhouse lights. Power for both is short this morning. The kid picks one | Responsibility | "Every choice costs something." |
| 4:30 | "Follow me!" PIP leads to the day's real task | Purpose | the task's name |

## 3. The shape of a played sol ☀️
A played sol is about 4 to 6 minutes. The kid chooses when to sleep; sleep is the skip.

1. **Wake, 06:30, inside HAB 1.** A fixed wake time, like real crews. The curtain opens, the HAB lights come up.
2. **Morning check.** The life support panel shows what the night used: oxygen, water, power. The kid sees the numbers
   that dropped while they slept. Breakfast and a drink are a quick use spot (motion and sound, the meters move).
3. **Mail from Earth.** Mission Control's message arrives. If the kid asked something yesterday, the answer arrives now,
   because a message takes minutes each way. The visor shows today's real delay.
4. **The plan.** One main task (outside) and one or two chores, picked from the menu below. PIP suggests, the kid decides.
5. **Suit check and airlock.** Three ticks on the visor, then the airlock cycle. It takes time, and it cannot be
   skipped: that is the lesson (no air outside).
6. **The outside task.** The heart of the sol: the beauty, the drive, the discovery. The suit's air limits how long the
   kid stays out, so the trip home is part of the plan.
7. **Back in.** Brush off the dust before the hatch (Mars dust is toxic), pick a seat, log what was found.
8. **Sunset.** Blue near the sun. PIP points it out the first time and never again.
9. **Sleep.** "Night" or "Nap 20 min". The time-lapse plays from a drone above the base: the sun sets, the stars and the
   two moons move. When sols are skipped, a card shows what the base used meanwhile.

## 4. The task menu 🧰
Each task is a use spot and a few props (most of the motion exists already; see `design/handoff/whats-left.md`).
**Learns** is what the kid's body or eyes find out before the word arrives.

| Task | Where | Learns (felt first) | Then named |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clean the solar panels | Solar farm | Power was falling for days; brushing lifts the meter at once | Dust storms ended Opportunity |
| Turn the oxygen valve | O2 tanks | The tank gauge drops while the maker rests | Our air comes from tanks and from splitting water |
| Plant, then pick lettuce | Greenhouse | Lettuce takes many sols; one harvest is a small salad | Plants cheer the crew; they don't feed it |
| Take rock samples | Near Mont Mercou | Layers in the rock, like pages | These layers formed in an ancient lake |
| Visit Curiosity | A drive away | A real robot that has been here for years | "Gale could once support life" (never "life was found") |
| Radio home | HAB 1 desk | The reply comes back much later | Light takes minutes to cross space |
| Fix a battery | Battery bank | A cold night drained more than expected | Heaters eat power at night |
| Shelter from a flare | Storm shelter | Walls of water bags around you | Water blocks radiation |
| Ride out a dust storm | Inside | Dark sky, panels give little, the kid rations | Choices: heat, air or plants |

## 5. Thirty sols without boredom 📅
- **Played sols.** About 6 to 8 sols are played in full. Each new played sol adds one new thing, so there is always
  something to discover (Sol 1 gravity and controls, Sol 2 power and dust, Sol 3 water and food, Sol 4 the radio delay
  and Curiosity, Sol 5 a solar flare, and so on).
- **Quiet sols.** Between them, sleep skips a few sols. The time-lapse shows the sky turning, and a card shows the base's
  ledger: oxygen, water, food, power used, panels dustier. The kid feels the budget without counting it.
- **Events** come from real data where we have it (labelled LIVE or ARCHIVED with its date), never at random only to
  punish.
- **Length.** A whole mission is about 30 minutes; the first five minutes carry the wow (section 2).
- **Replay.** A lost mission shows the three choices that caused it, then lets the kid try again from the start.

## 6. The last sol: going home 🚀
Pack the samples into the sample case, carry it to the ascent vehicle, climb in. The launch is seen from outside, then
from the window: the base shrinks, Gale shrinks, a bright dot that is Earth. The result card says what the samples can
tell Earth's labs, in one line a kid can repeat.

## 7. What a kid tells their parents afterwards 🗣️
1. "I could jump really high, because Mars pulls less."
2. "Messages to Earth take minutes, so we had to decide alone."
3. "Dust covered the solar panels and the power went down."
4. "We hid behind water when the sun threw out radiation."
5. "The sunset on Mars is blue."

## 8. Facts this page uses that still need a source ❓

- Phobos crosses the sky fast and rises in the west; Deimos is slow (orbit periods).
- Earth is visible from Mars as a bright evening or morning "star" (Curiosity's photo of Earth).
