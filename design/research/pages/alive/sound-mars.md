# Sound on Mars: NASA and JPL pages (excerpts)
Fetched 2026-11-12 with curl (default user agent), text pulled from the HTML. Quotes verbatim.

## NASA Science, "Sounds of Mars"
URL: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-2020-perseverance/sounds-of-mars/ (page last updated May 07, 2026)
- "If you were standing on Mars, you'd hear a quieter, more muffled version of what you'd hear on Earth, and you'd wait slightly longer to hear it."
- "the biggest change to audio would be to high-pitch sounds, higher than most voices. Some sounds that we're used to on Earth, like whistles, bells or bird songs, would almost be inaudible on Mars."
- "Mars has a lower speed of sound, around 540 mph (~240 meters per second), compared to about 760 mph (~340 meters per second) on Earth."
- "The Martian atmosphere is about 100 times less dense than on Earth ... On Mars you'd have to be much closer to the source of a sound to hear it at the same volume as you would on Earth."
- "The atmosphere of Mars, made up of 96 percent carbon dioxide, would absorb a lot of higher-pitched sounds, so only lower-pitched sounds would travel long distances. This effect is known as attenuation ... and it would be more noticeable the farther you were from the source."
- "Of course, if you found yourself on Mars, you'd have a spacesuit on."
- "Perseverance has captured sounds such as dust devils, the whir of the Ingenuity Mars Helicopter in flight, and the sound of its wheels crunching over the rocky Martian terrain."

## NASA/JPL, "What Sounds Captured by NASA's Perseverance Rover Reveal About Mars" (April 2022, Maurice et al. 2022 in Nature)
URL: https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/what-sounds-captured-by-nasas-perseverance-rover-reveal-about-mars/
- "the speed of sound is slower on the Red Planet than on Earth and that, mostly, a deep silence prevails."
- "low-pitched sounds travel at about 537 mph (240 meters per second), while higher-pitched sounds move at 559 mph (250 meters per second)."
- "On Earth, sound might drop off after about 213 feet (65 meters); on Mars, it falters at just 26 feet (8 meters), with high-pitched sounds being lost completely at that distance."
- Maurice: "At some point, we thought the microphone was broken, it was so quiet."
- Chide: "Mars is very quiet because of low atmospheric pressure ... But the pressure changes with the seasons on Mars."
- Ingenuity rotors: "a distinctive, low-pitched sound at 84 hertz"; the laser spark: "a high-pitched noise above 2 kilohertz."
- "the rover's mechanical whine and click in a light Martian wind; the whir of rotors on Ingenuity ...; the crackling strike of a rock-zapping laser."

## NASA Science resource, "Open Mic on Mars" (video transcript, Justin Maki, JPL)
URL: https://science.nasa.gov/resource/open-mic-on-mars/
- "Sounds on earth have very rich harmonics ... On Mars the atmosphere attenuates a lot of those higher frequencies. So you tend to hear the lower frequencies and it's a much more isolated sound, a little more muted than the sounds we hear on earth."
- Rover driving: "the rover's wheels are made of metal. So this metal is rolling over rocks and sand and it makes this really clanky, squeaky sound."
- "The Martian atmosphere can propagate sound a lot further than we thought it could."

## NASA Science resource, "Listen to NASA's Ingenuity Mars Helicopter in Flight"
URL: https://science.nasa.gov/resource/listen-to-nasas-ingenuity-mars-helicopter-in-flight/
- "With Perseverance parked 262 feet (80 meters) from the helicopter's takeoff and landing spot ... Even during flight when the helicopter's blades are spinning at 2,537 rpm, the sound is greatly muffled by the thin Martian atmosphere."
- "Scientists made the audio ... easier to hear by isolating the 84 hertz helicopter blade sound, reducing the frequencies below 80 hertz and above 90 hertz, and increasing the volume". So the public clip is processed, not what an ear would hear.

## NASA Science resource, "NASA's Perseverance Rover Microphone Captures Sounds From Mars"
URL: https://science.nasa.gov/resource/nasas-perseverance-rover-microphone-captures-sounds-from-mars/
- "recorded by the microphone on the side of NASA's Perseverance Rover on Feb. 20, 2021. In the first set, sounds from the rover itself dominate. In the second set, the sound was filtered to make sounds from Mars more audible. You can hear a little wind in the second set."
