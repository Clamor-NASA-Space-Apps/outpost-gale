Deployable and relocatable free-standing, solar arrays will be required for future lunar
South Pole missions. Major design requirements for these arrays will be low mass,
compact launch stowage, and highly reliable deployment and retraction performance. A
novel conceptual design referred to as the Relocatable Solar Array (RSA) is presented for
a 10 kW solar array to address these requirements. Simply stated, the concept is a pair of
solar cell blankets freely hanging from a horizontal cross arm that is supported by a
vertical slender telescoping mast, all of which, rests on a deployable tripod base. A major
factor in simplifying this array is that the force exerted by lunar gravity is used to deploy
and maintain the extension of the hanging array blankets. The other major factor in
achieving the desired low mass and high volumetric efficiency is that the resulting array
system operates in the vacuum, low-gravity, lunar environment with no deployed
vibration frequency requirement. Such an environment enables the use of extraordinarily
slender and low mass structural members to support the hanging array blankets. This
concept was developed, in part, to serve as a NASA reference solar array concept against
which other proposed arrays can be compared.
